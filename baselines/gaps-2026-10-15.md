## Baseline and profiles

Twenty-one native/Rust pairs per stage, two warmups, under benchmark mode:

| Model | Stage | Native median (ms) | Rust median (ms) | Rust / native |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| graph20-20-1rand | parse | 5.768 | 5.701 | 0.9884 |
| graph20-20-1rand | presolve | 4.353 | 4.747 | 1.0906 |
| graph20-20-1rand | matched presolved solve | 145.829 | 148.628 | 1.0192 |
| neos-1171737 | parse | 10.064 | 8.153 | 0.8101 |
| neos-1171737 | presolve | 7.851 | 8.287 | 1.0555 |
| neos-1171737 | matched presolved solve | 266.411 | 273.615 | 1.0270 |

Thus the remaining solve excesses are approximately 2.80 and 7.20 ms.
Presolve adds another ~0.4 ms on each model; parsing is not slower. The stage
adapter excludes parse/presolve/postsolve from the matched solve timer.

Separate six-second / 1-ms samples of repeated matched solves were taken for
both implementations on both models. Full local profiles are
`target/gaps-{model}-{rust,native}.sample.txt`; collapsed summaries are saved
in `gaps-2026-10-15-profiles.txt`.

On graph, Rust's top stacks include triangular transpose solves (748 samples),
Harris selection (554), hypersparse row-wise update (463), primal pricing
(430), and primal edge norms (340). Native splits several of these across
separate symbols: its Harris first pass has 382 samples, hypersparse update
443, and pricing candidate update 421. These are profile counts, not isolated
kernel timings; comparing a combined Rust symbol to one native subroutine
would overstate the difference.

On neos, regular row-wise update dominates Rust (977 samples versus native's
741), followed by primal edge norms and triangular solves. Native's and
Rust's stack distributions implicate several kernels rather than a single
extra algorithmic pass.

## Experiments and fidelity

1. **Harris candidate layout.** Pinned
   `revised_simplex.cc::ComputeHarrisRatioAndLeavingCandidates` and its second
   Harris pass retain `leaving_candidates_` as a `SparseColumn`. Rust used a
   `Vec<(RowIndex, f64)>`, with 16-byte interleaved entries on this target. The
   prototype reuses the existing `SparseColumn` implementation: 32-bit row
   entries and separate coefficient storage, in native insertion order. It
   retains both buffers between calls and adds no allocation per pivot after
   their capacities suffice. Candidate order, four ratio formulas, tolerances,
   tie-breaking, random draws, and deterministic clocks are unchanged. No new
   unsafe code or public signature change is introduced. This is an upstream
   representation repair, not a tolerance or termination adjustment.

   The first 31-pair Rust A/B gave candidate/baseline **0.9746x graph** and
   **0.9896x neos**, with exact status, iteration count, and objective bits.
   It remained provisional until the full-corpus gate, which later rejected it.

2. **Validated signed row pointer.** In regular row-wise accumulation, a
   prototype used the already-validated transpose's signed row offset
   directly rather than converting through the checked public index helper.
   It preserves the checked fallback for invalid public input, but changed
   LLVM's loop form (281 instruction lines versus the baseline's 203). A
   31-pair A/B against the Harris candidate was 1.0010x graph and **1.0282x
   neos**. Reverted. Fewer explicit checks do not imply a faster loop.

3. **Private filtered-row width.** Native's filtered left-inverse positions
   are `vector<ColIndex>`; Rust stores `usize`. A prototype changed only this
   private list to `ColIndex`, preserving all public APIs and support order.
   A 31-pair A/B against the Harris candidate was 1.0006x graph and **1.0355x
   neos**. Reverted. The width difference alone is insufficient evidence for
   a retained repair; the extra checked index conversions/code generation
   need separate consideration.

The two row-wise prototypes above are not retained and their timing is not
credited as an improvement. The previously rejected iterator/counting-loop
rewrites remain reverted as well.

## Final gates and decision

The direct SparseColumn candidate passed formatting, strict workspace Clippy,
full workspace tests, release fast-96 and six-extra-model pivot fixtures,
the qap12 primal precision/refactorization fixture, public fast-96 postsolve
and independent KKT checks, and all 21 pinned native/Rust presolved-state
fingerprints. The latter cover basis, variable values, reduced costs,
deterministic clocks, and refactorization counts. Local logs are
`target/gaps-{clippy,workspace-tests,trajectories,kkt,checkpoints}.log`.

A fresh 31-pair native comparison of the direct SparseColumn candidate gave:

| Model | Native median (ms) | Candidate median (ms) | Candidate / native |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| graph20-20-1rand | 146.564 | 145.219 | 0.990823 |
| neos-1171737 | 267.780 | 269.855 | 1.007748 |

These are candidate measurements, not retained performance claims. Separate
baseline and native runs have some desktop variation; the paired Rust A/B
comparison is the direct evidence of change-specific effects.

The required full public-pipeline nmm0-50k campaign used all 213 models, five
alternating timed pairs per model, one warmup per binary, and a 60-second
safety cap. Parse and output are outside the timer; presolve/simplex/postsolve
are inside. The harness calls pre-change Rust `native` and the candidate
`rust`; this full campaign is a Rust A/B, not a new full native-GLOP campaign.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Before sum of model medians | 23.163358 s |
| Candidate sum of model medians | 23.111027 s |
| Candidate / before summed time | 0.997741 |
| Median model ratio | 1.000741 |
| sqrt(nnz)-weighted geometric ratio | 0.999519 |
| Completed / wall-limited | 213 / 0 |
| Exact status/iterations/objective-bit A/B outcomes | 1,065 / 1,065 |

The ~0.23% aggregate gain does not erase per-model regressions. Initial
excesses above 1 ms were CVS (+26.35 ms, 1.01571x), dfl001 (+9.06 ms,
1.00304x), degen3 (+1.71 ms, 1.01443x), qap8 (+1.66 ms, 1.01397x), and
reblock115 (+1.06 ms, 1.01178x). All five were rechecked with eleven pairs
and one warmup; raw full data are in `gaps-2026-10-15-nmm-public-ab.json`.

A separate candidate put the same SparseColumn behind a Box to reduce the
inline workspace metadata footprint (Rust's sparse vector owns two Vecs).
This changes one allocation per workspace lifetime, not per pivot. Eleven
public A/B pairs measured 1.01027x CVS (+17.16 ms), 0.98956x graph (-1.61 ms),
and 0.98198x neos (-5.10 ms). It still regresses CVS and is rejected; metadata
placement alone does not account for the tradeoff. Raw data:
`gaps-2026-10-15-boxed-public-focused.json`.

The independent eleven-pair outlier recheck gave:

| Model | Candidate / before | Excess (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|
| cvs16r128-89 | 1.018515 | +31.005 |
| dfl001 | 0.999695 | -0.912 |
| degen3 | 0.991768 | -0.987 |
| qap8 | 0.999066 | -0.110 |
| reblock115 | 1.000289 | +0.026 |

All 55 recheck pairs have identical status, iterations, and objective bits.
The other apparent regressions do not repeat; **CVS does**, at +31.0 ms
(1.85%). The direct layout therefore fails the retention gate. The boxed
variant also regresses CVS and fails the focused prerequisite; it was not
advanced to another full campaign.

**Final decision: all production prototypes reverted.** The production-source
diff under `glop/src` and `lp_data/src` is empty relative to `2f493f2`.
The original implementation is retained. Only the investigation report, raw
artifacts, and plan/ledger updates remain. Earlier target-only improvements
are recorded as rejected experiment results, not improvements to the final
workspace. No public API, tolerance, safety check, or numerical fixture was
changed in the final tree.

The small graph/neos baseline gaps remain. The profiles support further
work on the Harris and row-wise kernels, but these concrete representation
repairs demonstrate why source fidelity and local timings alone cannot
establish a globally beneficial optimization. The Rust sparse-column type
uses separate Vec capacities, whereas pinned native uses shared sparse-vector
storage; that implementation difference is a follow-up hypothesis, not a
proven explanation of the CVS regression.

Benchmark mode was already on at entry and remains on; no restoration was
requested or performed. The shared restore journal is preserved. Formatting
and an empty production diff are verified after reverting.

Peak RSS was also sampled once per model/binary, including parsing and
presolve, after the timing campaigns. The rejected direct candidate's raw
values are in `gaps-2026-10-15-memory.json`; startup-sensitive single samples
do not establish a memory improvement. Refactorization counts and numerical
quality are covered by the exact checkpoint and public KKT gates above;
no isolated LU-factorization speedup is claimed.
