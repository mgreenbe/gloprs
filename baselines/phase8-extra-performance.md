# Phase 8: MIPLIB 2017 and Mittelmann LPopt timing baseline

This is the first repeatable serial comparison of pinned upstream GLOP and
`gloprs` on the shared MIPLIB 2017 and Mittelmann LPopt datasets. Selection is
in [`extra-lp-corpus.json`](extra-lp-corpus.json): 166 non-Netlib MPS models
with **fewer than 150,000 constraint-matrix entries**, 130 from MIPLIB and
36 from Mittelmann. The shared source manifests also count objective
coefficients, so the selection was generated by checksum-verifying candidate
files and checking both native and Rust parser dimensions and matrix nnz.
MIPLIB instances are solved as LP relaxations, not as mixed-integer programs.

## Method and scope

- Apple M2, macOS 15.6.1; Rust 1.95.0; Apple clang 17.0.0; pinned OR-Tools
  commit `100f66e6242ab8bf8d32feb8f3bf086db66ae2b5`.
- Native reference adapters: C++20, `-O2 -DNDEBUG`; Rust: Cargo release with
  one codegen unit and ThinLTO. The adapters and release examples were built
  from the working tree based on `a9df5b8`; exact executable SHA-256 values
  are listed below because the 60-second harness extension was uncommitted
  during measurement.
- One native and one Rust warmup per model and stage, then three serial
  alternating pairs (`N-R`, `R-N`, `N-R`). No other benchmark ran concurrently.
  The tables sum **per-model medians**; raw trial times and outcomes are in
  [`phase8-extra-stages.json`](phase8-extra-stages.json) and
  [`phase8-extra-public.json`](phase8-extra-public.json).
- Parse includes reading MPS and conversion to the solver model. Presolve starts from a
  parsed model and includes its copy, near-zero cleanup, and preprocessing.
  Matched-presolved solve excludes parse, presolve, and postsolve. Public
  timing starts from a parsed model and includes preprocessing, simplex, and
  postsolve. File loading is outside the presolve, solve, and public timers;
  trace printing is outside every internal timer.
  The per-solve wall limit is 60 seconds.
- Native/Rust parse dimensions and presolve shapes/status match on all 166.
  Every comparable timed pair agrees on solver status, iteration count, and
  objective (within the existing `1e-8` comparison). An earlier full-corpus
  differential pass checked vectors and basis statuses on 162 models at
  20 seconds; separate 60-second checks cover `satellites2-60-fs` and the
  newly eligible `fome12`, for 164 non-wall-limited public results. This is not a
  strict pivot-by-pivot trajectory fixture for the new corpus.
  `tools/validate_extra_lp_baseline.py` provides an opt-in Rust-only
  regression gate against these pinned native outcomes.

## Outlier follow-up (working tree after `2ce68ff`)

Paired macOS stack samples localized `neos-1445765` presolve to
`DoubletonFreeColumnPreprocessor`, especially its ordered sparse-vector merge.
Rust had been building an array of entry pairs and then copying it into its
index and coefficient arrays. The pinned C++ implementation builds the two
arrays directly in a temporary sparse vector and swaps it into place. The
Rust port now does likewise and uses the upstream multiply-then-add order.
With 100 presolve repetitions, one warmup, and three alternating trial pairs,
the Rust/native ratio fell from 1.956x to 1.484x. A fresh three-pair public
run measured 1.416x on this model, versus 1.732x in the pinned baseline.

The same profile campaign identified dense permutation in LU BTRAN and
symbolic triangular reachability as hot on `piperout-27`. Iterating the two
permutation inputs together eliminates their redundant bounds checks without
unsafe code, and symbolic reach now traverses row-index storage directly,
without loading coefficients. In a five-model, ten-solve-per-trial comparison,
the aggregate matched-solve ratio changed from 1.266x to 1.249x after the
permutation edit. The subsequent seven-model, five-solve-per-trial run measured
1.298x for `piperout-27` and 1.219x for `unitcal_7`; these remain substantial
regressions. A packed-bitset symbolic-workspace experiment did not improve
timing and was removed.

| `fhnw-binpack4-48` | 1.582x | 1.535x |
| `pg` | 1.483x | 1.434x |
| `decomp2` | 1.378x | 1.363x |

Raw follow-up results and stack samples are ignored under `target/phase8-extra-*`.
The complete 164-model pinned-native status/iteration/objective gate and the
strict fast-96 Netlib trajectory fixture passed after these edits. The
remaining short-model ratios are **not resolved**; sampled `fhnw-binpack4-48`
still spends substantial Rust time in basis refactorization, while `bnatt500`
and `piperout-27` emphasize BTRAN/edge-norm work. These need isolated paired
kernel timing before a claim of parity.

## Pricing and bounds-check follow-up

A further 500-solve paired sample of `fhnw-binpack4-48` confirmed that
refactorization is a significant cost in **both** solvers. It accounted for
about 798 of 4,878 Rust main-thread samples and 662 of 5,122 native samples;
this alone cannot explain the full solve ratio. Other sampled work includes
primal edge norms, pricing, and ratio tests. Isolated live-basis timings are
needed before changing Markowitz.

Upstream `PrimalPrices` uses a branchless conditional XOR of two bitset
memberships to test dual infeasibility. Rust now uses the same operation for
full and incremental primal price updates, with a word-boundary unit test.
In a fresh six-model, five-solve-per-trial comparison, matched-solve ratios
were `fhnw-binpack4-48` 1.633x, `pg` 1.507x, `bnatt500` 1.721x,
`bnatt400` 1.683x, `piperout-27` 1.300x, and `unitcal_7` 1.218x.
The previous comparable run measured 1.645x, 1.502x, 1.745x, 1.719x,
1.298x, and 1.219x respectively. The small improvement is mostly confined
to the `bnatt` pair; this is a fidelity fix, not parity. Raw results are
ignored at `target/phase8-extra-pricing-xor.json`.

An unchecked-index variant of the sparse common-index merge did **not**
improve `neos-1445765` presolve (1.491x versus about 1.486x with checked
reads) and was removed. Workspace tests, strict fast-96 Netlib trajectories,
and the 164-model pinned-native extra-corpus outcome gate pass with the
pricing edit.

For `fhnw-binpack4-48`, 28 captured refactorization basis matrices give an
isolated LU sum-of-times ratio of 2.208x Rust/native (three paired trials,
10 repetitions per matrix after warmup). These very short square-matrix
probes total only about 2 ms native per trial and do not capture the live
selected compact-matrix view, so they are a localization signal, not a
full-solve attribution. GLOP's residual singleton-column path copies its
source column in one pass. Removing Rust's redundant pivot lookup and
per-entry pivot-state filtering brings that path into alignment; the isolated
ratio after the change is 2.181x, a small difference. GLOP also reuses its
initial singleton entry buffer and marks claimed rows in the row permutation;
Rust now does the same instead of allocating a fresh entry list and bitmap
per refactorization. The one-pass path passed 1,000 native LU differential
traces, strict fast-96 Netlib trajectories, and the 164-model extra-corpus
outcome gate. The residual LU gap remains unresolved.

The presolved selected-view probe mode now records the actual compact source
and basis selections for this model. Five serial paired trials, with 100
factorizations per selection and retained LU objects, measured 1.968–2.059x
Rust/native on the 28 views before the latest changes. Matching GLOP's
libc++ singleton-entry sort lowered that range to 1.912–1.939x. Reusing
Markowitz's temporary row/column permutations and LU's output permutations
instead of allocating them for each refactorization retained a 1.913–1.990x
range. These are small improvements, not a resolution. A five-trial,
20-solve-per-trial paired presolved-solve run measured 1.631x after the edits,
versus 1.658x in the pinned stage baseline. The 28 LU calls account for only
roughly 2 ms of the roughly 11 ms per-solve gap, so further investigation
must include the per-pivot numerical paths, not just Markowitz.
The first per-pivot mismatch found was the Phase-I leaving-row ratio test:
native heapifies breakpoints in O(k) and pops only until the improvement is
exhausted, while Rust sorted every breakpoint in O(k log k). A reusable
binary heap with GLOP's ratio/magnitude/row comparison lowers the five-trial
presolved-solve ratio from 1.625x (after a separate sparse direction-norm
traversal fix) to 1.575x. The latter also removes an unnecessary full-row
norm scan after the direction support is known. The end-to-end gap is still
material and remains open.
Recycling the pivot permutation buffers through the retained LU object gives
a clean selected-view LU range of 1.897–1.908x and a final five-trial
presolved-solve ratio of 1.572x. The 164-model corpus outcome gate passed
before this final storage-only change; strict fast-96 Netlib trajectories and
the pinned `fhnw` outcome also passed after it.

A subsequent five-trial paired check found that clearing the unit-row inverse
both in `UpdateRow` and in `LeftSolveForUnitRow` was redundant; likewise the
retained right-update scratchpad was cleared both after one replacement and
before the next. Removing those extra clears matches upstream's lifecycle but
does not explain the slowdown: ratios remained 1.572x and 1.574x in successive
20-solve-per-trial comparisons. Saved initial-basis FTRAN probes show a 1.694x
relative ratio on 3,739 right-hand sides, but only about 0.15 ms absolute
difference for the entire batch. An unchecked-read trial in the packed
rank-one dot product measured 1.570x and was reverted because the difference
was noise. The remaining `fhnw` gap is not yet at parity.

An isolated LU sample identified a larger upstream-fidelity difference:
`TriangularMatrix::Reset()` pre-sizes the diagonal, starts, and pruning vectors
in GLOP, whereas Rust previously appended to all three on every completed
column. After adopting indexed writes with a separate completed-column count,
five serial selected-view LU trials measured about 1.70x Rust/native, versus
1.90x before. A matched five-trial presolved-solve run with the ordinary
release build measured 0.017695 s native and 0.021492 s Rust per solve
(**1.215x**), down from roughly 1.57x. A machine-target Rust build measured
1.218x; after this storage change it does not meaningfully outperform the
portable release build. The remaining approximately 3.8 ms per solve is
unresolved, including about 1.4 ms attributable to the 28 selected-view LU
calls at their isolated median timings.
An independent nine-trial, 50-solve-per-trial serial confirmation measured
0.017662 s native and 0.021454 s Rust per solve (1.215x); the status,
3,774 iterations, and objective bits agreed in every trial. Strict fast-96
trajectories and the full 164-model extra-corpus outcome gate also passed.

The next selected-view LU pass matched native's constant-time compact-view
empty test, two-pass residual-pattern row reservation, and diagonal-only true
singleton factor columns. Retaining each singleton coefficient alongside
32-bit row/column indices in the sort buffer avoids a second compact-column
lookup and matches GLOP's `MatrixEntry` layout. On the same 28 chronological
`fhnw-binpack4-48` selections, the seven-trial Rust/native ratio moved from
1.69x to 1.54x; five additional paired trials with compact scratch indices
spanned 1.532–1.555x (sum-of-medians 1.534x). This is a real improvement but
not LU parity. A five-second Rust sample after the change put roughly 1,002
of 4,300 samples in triangular-factor transposition and 595 in column close;
the largest 2,398-sample bucket is inlined Markowitz setup/work and still
needs finer localization. The strict fast-96 trajectory gate, all workspace
tests, and the pinned `fhnw` outcome/3,774-iteration check passed. The old
1.215x whole-solve timing has not yet been remeasured after this pass.
After the one-read singleton scan and row-only structural traversal, seven
paired trials with 500 repetitions per selection measured 1.514–1.548x
Rust/native (sum-of-medians 1.520x). A fresh five-pair matched-presolved
solve check measured 0.017641 s native and 0.021063 s Rust per solve, or
1.194x, down from 1.215x. Temporary Rust stage timers over 7,000
refactorizations attributed 243 ms to true-singleton extraction, 217 ms to
residual-singleton extraction, 68 ms to residual-pattern construction, 12 ms
to setup, and 1.4 ms to numerical elimination; factor transpose was outside
these stages. The timer code was removed after localization. Native stage
timings are not yet measured, so the next repair should still be checked by
paired full-factor timings and differential gates.
Aligning retained transpose population with native's storage lifecycle left
the seven-trial selected-view ratio at about 1.52x; its benefit is removing
non-upstream workspace clearing, not a measured speedup. A repeated
dimension-changing transpose/solve test covers the changed lifecycle.
Eliminating Rust's extra pivot-order vectors during Markowitz extraction then
improved the seven-trial, 500-repetition selected-view LU ratio to 1.298–1.328x
(sum-of-medians 1.317x) from about 1.52x. Native GLOP writes direct row/column
permutations during extraction and populates their inverses afterward; Rust
now does the same, transferring and reusing direct-permutation buffers rather
than writing pivot-order vectors and inverting them again during LU install.
A five-pair, 20-solve-per-trial matched-presolved `fhnw` check measured
0.017625 s native and 0.020575 s Rust per solve (1.167x, down from 1.194x).
The remaining LU and whole-solve gaps are open.
Line-level sampling identified Rust's adjacent-index prefix sum in
`TriangularMatrix::transpose_into()` as a major cost. A forward mutable-slice
prefix pass is algebraically and structurally identical to GLOP's transpose
population, but avoids repeated checked indexing. Seven paired trials at 500
repetitions per selected view measured 1.128–1.135x Rust/native
(sum-of-medians 1.131x), down from 1.317x. A five-pair, 20-solve-per-trial
matched-presolved check measured 0.017673 s native and 0.020259 s Rust per
solve (1.146x), down from 1.167x. This is close but not parity; the remaining
roughly 13% isolated LU gap remained at that point. Tracking nonfinite entries
in compact storage removes the repeated full validation scan on ordinary
finite inputs without changing the `NonFinite` error for a selected bad
column. Seven paired, 500-repetition selected-view trials now range from
1.021x to 1.031x (sum-of-medians 1.025x); raw data are in
`target/fhnw-lu-finite-metadata-raw.csv`. Five matched-presolved whole-solve
trials measure 0.017690 s native and 0.019891 s Rust per solve (1.124x), in
`target/fhnw-stage-solve-finite-metadata.json`. The isolated LU gap is now
about 2.5%, while the larger whole-solve gap remains to be localized.
An eight-second paired sampling pass on repeated `fhnw-binpack4-48` direct
presolved solves found substantial primal edge-norm BTRAN and primal-phase
loop work on both sides. The existing isolated dense rank-one solve benchmark
with 32 updates and 4–16 entries was faster in Rust (0.79–0.86x native), so
its presence in the sample was not sufficient evidence of a rank-one kernel
regression. GLOP retains the primal-residual scratchpad while the Rust port
allocated it on every correction check; Rust now retains and clears that
vector. Seven paired trials with 50 solves each measured 0.017647 s native
and 0.019823 s Rust per solve (1.123x), showing negligible timing effect.
Raw data are in `target/fhnw-stage-solve-residual-reuse.json`.

`miplib2017/enlight_hard` was solved completely by presolve, so it is
inapplicable to matched-presolved simplex timing but included in the public
result. `miplib2017/rmatr200-p5` and
`mittelmann-lpopt/nug08-3rd` remained nonterminal at 60 seconds on both
solvers; their capped iterations and objectives differ, as expected from a
wall-clock cutoff. They are retained in the corpus and raw artifacts but
excluded from solve/public aggregates and numerical-parity claims.
An additional one-pair public probe with a 180-second limit still left both
nonterminal: `rmatr200-p5` reported `INIT` on both sides at approximately
180 s, and `nug08-3rd` reported `PRIMAL_FEASIBLE` on both sides (Rust's
time-limit check returned at 181.9 s). These are censored observations, not
evidence of a near-1.0x completed-solve ratio. The ignored raw probe is
`target/phase8-extra-long-probes.json`.
`miplib2017/neos-3656078-kumeu` returned `IMPRECISE` on both sides with
identical iterations and objective bits in all three public trials; the other
163 comparable public cases returned `OPTIMAL`.
