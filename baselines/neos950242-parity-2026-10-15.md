# neos-950242: transpose-cache fidelity repair — 15 October 2026

The 15.3% public-solve deficit was primarily presolve allocation churn. Returning the moved-out stale transpose to LinearProgram for lazy rebuilding closes it: final 31-pair native/Rust medians are **71.680 / 68.041 ms**, **0.94922x** (Rust 5.08% faster). All 31 outcome pairs are exact. The retained change preserves upstream buffer lifetime; it does not alter elimination arithmetic, pass order, tolerances, or pivot selection.

## Cause and implementation

The baseline stage check found native/Rust presolve at 35.040/42.487 ms (1.2126x), matched simplex at 35.675/36.403 ms (1.0204x), and parsing at 25.735/25.128 ms. Thus most of the roughly 10.76-ms public gap was in presolve.

In pinned `DoubletonEqualityRowPreprocessor::Run`, original_transpose is a reference to the LinearProgram cache. Mutating primary columns invalidates it, but its sparse-column allocations survive and are reused by the next PopulateFromTranspose. Rust moves this cache out to satisfy ownership while mutating bounds and columns. It restored the cache only for an unchanged pass; after substitutions it dropped all its sparse-column storage. Repeated passes therefore freed and reallocated the transpose.

The retained fix returns the stale cache after a changed pass, marks it inconsistent, and lets the existing lazy transpose builder refill it. The original early abnormal-return path is unchanged. An unchanged pass still restores a consistent transpose. A regression verifies that recycled stale data is rebuilt after the primary matrix changes. No unsafe code, parameter or numerical changes.

Six-second, 1-ms serial samples of repeated presolve show substantial allocator work. After repair, top-stack tiny_malloc_from_free_list samples fell from 390 to 266, tiny_free_no_lock from 319 to 233, and free_tiny from 246 to 181. Sample counts describe separate runs, not exact allocation counts. [Collapsed profiles](neos950242-profiles-2026-10-15.txt).

The first prototype stage measurement put presolve at 31.482 ms versus native 35.211 ms (0.8941x). Isolated simplex still had a small 1.049x difference in that pass, so the claim is public-pipeline parity, not parity in every substage. A 31-pair before/after public comparison measured 77.844/67.878 ms (0.8720x, 12.80% faster). Final native repeats measured 0.95556x and 0.94922x.

## Retention and tradeoffs

Two full 213-model nmm0-50k A/B campaigns used five timed pairs per model and a 60-second per-solve limit. Both completed without limits and with all 1,065 pairs bit-exact each. The first prototype aggregate was 0.99399x; the final source aggregate was **0.995293x**, 23.152152 s before / 23.043172 s after (0.47% less time). Final median-model ratio 0.996565x; sqrt(nnz)-weighted geometric ratio 0.992603x. These are sums of per-model medians, not campaign wall time.

This is not a uniform per-model speedup. The initial full run and focused recheck found repeatable small costs on peg-solitaire-a3, CMS750_4, and truss. Preserving the original abnormal-return placement did not remove every cost. Stage checks showed essentially unchanged presolve for all three; isolated solves were 1.0074x peg, 0.9979x CMS, and 0.9993x truss. Do not attribute their entire public differences to extra presolve work.

The final full run had five apparent excesses above 1 ms. All were rechecked with 15 timed pairs, two warmups; all 75 outcomes exact. Final recheck medians:

| Model | After/before | Excess ms |
|---|---:|---:|
| CMS750_4 | 1.01209 | +8.783 |
| peg-solitaire-a3 | 1.00406 | +1.466 |
| greenbeb | 1.00299 | +0.414 |
| 80bau3b | 1.00743 | +0.832 |
| truss | 1.00134 | +0.235 |

Retain the upstream storage-lifetime repair for the large target improvement, repeatable public parity, and aggregate improvement in both full campaigns, with the small costs above explicitly recorded. In particular, CMS750_4 remains a repeatable roughly 1% tradeoff. Unlike the prior loop-only prototypes, this also repairs a concrete repeated-allocation divergence from upstream.
